Bottom line

The offer you described to the Bowen family and the formal Letter of Intent attached to it are two different deals. The narrative promises the family keeps ownership through their own LLC, gets paid in installments, and shares in the profit. The signed document says you personally buy the house outright for $400,000 with a bank mortgage. Before this goes to the family, we need to settle which one it actually is.

The core conflict

You wrote that the attached LOI formalizes Offer 1. Read side by side, it doesn't — it reads like a standard cash purchase, not a joint venture.

Other gaps worth flagging

Even taken on its own, the LOI is missing terms a family (or their attorney) will ask about.

Gap

No loan terms anywhere

Every version of this deal runs on "my investor/lender," but no interest rate, term, or security is ever stated — not for the $300k mortgage in the LOI, not for the private financing in Offer 1 or 2. This is the one place the family's own attorney will push hardest, especially given the whole pitch is built on not repeating what happened to other families.

Gap

Offer 2's numbers don't resolve

"Pay an immediate $60,000 … take $250,000 out of Woodside" — is the $60k part of the $250k, or on top of it? As written, it can be read either way, and the two readings put very different amounts of debt on the house.

Gap

Woodside as collateral isn't disclosed

Pulling $250,000 in equity to fund a DC flip means Woodside stands behind that loan. If the flip underperforms, the family's home is what's exposed. The document never says this plainly — it should, given the story you lead with is about protecting people from exactly that outcome.

Gap

No financing or inspection contingency

Section 3 commits to a $300,000 mortgage; Section 4 is titled "Inspection and Due Diligence" but waives all contingencies. If the mortgage doesn't come through, there's no stated way out.

Lower stakes — these don't touch deal terms, so I can clean them up regardless of how the questions above resolve.

"Acrchitect" in Section 2

Typo, but also worth a second look: releasing an $8,000 earnest deposit to an architect is unusual. Likely meant to be the attorney or escrow/title company of record.

Fix

It's named as one of three key comps but only appears in the table. Adding the number in the paragraph makes the CMA read as more complete.

Fix

CMA leans on two outlier sales

Boyden (33% over ask) and Fernwood (31% over ask) anchor the $850–925k ARV against a comp spread of $700k–$1M. Not wrong, but the framing could read as more conservative so it holds up to scrutiny.

Before this goes out

Confirm which structure this is actually meant to be — the joint venture where the family keeps Woodside through their own LLC, or a straight $400,000 purchase by Marlon. Once that's settled, the loan terms and the Offer 2 numbers can be filled in and the whole thing rewritten so it says one consistent thing.
